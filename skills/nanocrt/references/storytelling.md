# Storytelling reference

**Goal:** turn script narration from a flat sequence of facts into a story the
viewer feels — the difference between "information dump" and "content people
share". Loaded by Phase 2 (outline) and Phase 3 (script), driven by the Phase 0
preferences `story_structure`, `universal_value`, and `foreshadowing`.

Source: `handbook_storytelling.md` (distilled; detail in context.md reference
inventory).

---

## Two structures

Pick one at Phase 0. They are the same bones in different clothes — On-a-Day is
the Five-Part structure reordered for everyday telling.

### Five-Part Structure

| # | Part | Job | Checklist |
|---|------|-----|-----------|
| 1 | **Introduction** | Make the viewer curious. Give enough concrete context that they can "relate" before the story starts. | Does the viewer know why this matters to *them*? |
| 2 | **Rising Action** | Problem after problem, tension climbing. Not one problem — a ladder. | Are there ≥2 escalating problems, not a flat list? |
| 3 | **Climax** | The peak tension — most surprising or most tense moment. | Is there one unmistakable peak, not a plateau? |
| 4 | **Falling Action / Resolution** | The problem starts to resolve. Answer appears but is not fully clear yet. | Does tension drop and the solution begin? |
| 5 | **Conclusion** | Close with a memorable lesson or takeaway. | Does the viewer walk away with one line they remember? |

### On-a-Day Structure

| # | Beat | Job |
|---|------|-----|
| 1 | **Dulu / Pada suatu hari** | Setting & context — the "before" state. |
| 2 | **Setiap hari** | The annoying/painful routine that repeats. |
| 3 | **Tetapi suatu hari** | The change or problem arrives (climax tension starts). |
| 4 | **Maka / Karena itu** | Action taken + result (resolution). |
| 5 | **Sejak saat itu** | New status + conclusion. |

Note: in On-a-Day, climax and resolution live inside "Tetapi suatu hari" through
"Maka" — the beat is the pivot, not the routine.

## Relatability — universal value

Viewers relate to the **universal value**, not the specific detail. The detail
makes it memorable; the value makes it relatable. Named value types:

- **Zero to Hero** — from failing to succeeding, low to high.
- **Underdog** — put down, then proved itself.
- **Transformation** — drastic change (insecure → confident).
- **Redemption** — from mistake to lesson, loss to finding.

Rule of thumb: if a beat can only mean something to people who share the exact
same experience, it needs a universal value stated on top of it. Avoid generic
vapor ("I worked hard and got rich") — pair the value with a concrete, specific
detail.

## Foreshadowing (optional, when Phase 0 `foreshadowing` = on)

Plant a clue early that only makes sense when the ending lands. The payoff moment
is when the viewer says "OH — I remember that." Adds perceived craft and re-watch
value.

Two flavors:

1. **Structural** — a detail shown early (an empty jar, a throwaway line) that
   the ending recontextualizes.
2. **Explicit/modern** — telling the viewer up front that a surprise exists:
   "Number 7 will change how you think about X." This is a promise to honor in the
   climax.

Rules when enabled:

- The clue must be plantable *before* the climax scene, with room for payoff.
- The payoff must explicitly reference the clue, so the connection is felt.
- A clue that cannot be paid off in the outline must be dropped — never planted
  and forgotten.

## Ethics boundary

Storytelling is a tool that can manipulate feelings; keep it on the ethical side
of the line:

- **Tujuannya untuk kebaikan** — purpose benefits the audience, not just the
  teller.
- **Tidak merugikan orang lain** — no harm to others.
- **Jujur dan transparan** — never invent facts or hide material information to
  make the story land. This supersedes everything above: a perfect story arc does
  not excuse an unverified claim.
